Preparing for a criminal defense meeting

Create a factual timeline while events are still fresh. Separate what you personally observed from what someone else told you. If you are uncertain about a time, location, or sequence, mark that uncertainty rather than filling in the blank.

Gather materials that may help the attorney review your account:

  • Tickets, summonses, charging papers, and court notices.
  • Documents describing release conditions or existing orders.
  • A list of scheduled appearances and other stated dates.
  • Names and contact details for potential witnesses.
  • Relevant messages, photographs, receipts, or other existing records.
  • Questions about employment, transportation, or family responsibilities affected by the case.

Ask how the firm wants to receive sensitive materials before sending them electronically. Preserve original records and explain where each item came from. A short, organized account can make the meeting more productive than a large collection of unexplained screenshots.

Mistakes that can complicate defense preparation

Avoid posting your account of events on social media or debating the accusation in public comments. Before discussing case details with other people, ask your attorney how those conversations could affect the matter. The same caution applies to trying to resolve a dispute directly with someone involved.

Do not delete messages, alter photographs, or edit documents that relate to the events. If you discover an error in your own notes, record a correction separately. Tell the attorney about material that seems unfavorable as well as information you believe supports your account.

Never assume an appointment with a law firm changes a court date or an existing instruction. Ask specifically about any scheduling concern and keep the original notice accessible. If you do not understand a condition listed in your paperwork, flag the exact language for review.
